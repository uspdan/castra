Self-hosted cyber range · authoring SDK

A range you run yourself, and fill with your own drills.

Castra deploys inside your own network and runs realistic red-team and blue-team exercises in isolated, hardened containers. The part that matters: your team authors and changes the exercises, with an SDK that needs no platform installed.

The gap

A team shopping for a range today finds three options, and each fails a different way.

Hosted SaaS

Holds your content and your results on someone else's servers. Regulated and air-gapped teams often can't use it, and no catalogue contains your log formats or your last incident.

CTF scoreboards

Check flags and stop there. Workload isolation, blue-team exercises and authoring tooling are left to you, so every challenge becomes a bespoke ops project.

In-house ranges

Built once by one enthusiast, then left to rot the moment that person moves on.

None lets a team own the platform and the content at once. That combination is the whole product.

The authoring loop

You do not need the platform to write a drill.

The SDK is a standalone Python package. One command scaffolds a working exercise; a second tests it with the same engine the platform runs, so what passes on your laptop loads cleanly on any deployment.

An exercise is a manifest, some evidence files, and its own test cases. It lives in your repository and is reviewed like the rest of your code. The team that worked an incident last month can drill the whole SOC on it next month.

What makes it different

Four decisions, held to.

i

Authoring is the core workflow

The SDK ships beside the platform and carries the same test coverage. Most competitors bolt an editor onto a fixed catalogue.

ii

Self-hosted, by design

Exercise content, results and telemetry stay on your network — because there is no server of ours to receive them.

iii

The blue team is first-class

Detection and forensics validators, ATT&CK coverage mapping, chain-of-custody exercises. Not just "pop the box".

iv

Drills become audit evidence

One request exports an evidence pack from the tamper-evident ledger: participants, timeline, a recomputable fingerprint. Built for the exercise-proof an auditor asks for.
